## Advanced Economic Simulation

### Dynamic Market Economics

The economy is no longer static. Prices fluctuate based on supply and demand:

- **Supply & Demand Curves**: Oversupply lowers prices; scarcity raises them
- **Seasonal Fluctuations**: Agricultural goods are cheaper at harvest, expensive in winter
- **Trade Route Competition**: Multiple cities trading the same goods affects prices
- **Market Crashes**: Rare events that can devastate your economy—diversify!

**Strategy Tip**: Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Diversified economies weather market crashes better.

### Resource Quality System

Not all resources are created equal:

- **Quality Levels**: Poor → Average → Good → Excellent → Luxury
- **Degradation**: Food spoils, materials weather if stored too long
- **Artisan Goods**: Master craftsmen produce superior items worth more
- **Reputation**: High-quality goods build your city's trade reputation

**Example**: A master potter produces luxury ceramics worth 3x standard pottery, but requires an apprenticeship system and proper workshop conditions.

### Advanced Taxation

Taxation is now a delicate balance:

- **Progressive Tax Brackets**: Tax the wealthy more without angering them
- **Tax Evasion**: Wealthier citizens are better at hiding income; hire inspectors
- **Corruption**: Reduces tax collection efficiency; build courts and maintain law
- **Special Taxes**: Luxury tax, inheritance tax, property tax, business licenses
- **Tariffs**: Set import/export duties to protect local industries or encourage trade

**Warning**: Over-taxation leads to tax evasion, emigration, and even revolt!

### Banking & Finance

Manage your city's finances like a modern economy:

- **Loans**: Borrow from Rome or private banks when cash-strapped (interest applies!)
- **Credit Rating**: Late payments increase future interest rates
- **Investments**: Fund merchant ventures or infrastructure bonds for returns
- **Inflation/Deflation**: Your monetary policy affects purchasing power
- **Default Consequences**: Miss loan payments? Expect seizure of assets or imperial intervention

**Advanced Strategy**: Take loans during economic downturns when interest is low, invest during booms.

## Complex Citizen Behavior

### Individual Citizens with Personalities

Citizens are now unique individuals:

- **Personality Traits**: Ambitious, lazy, loyal, rebellious, innovative, traditional, etc.
- **Memory**: Citizens remember disasters, golden ages, and historical events
- **Relationships**: Family networks, friendships, rivalries affect behavior
- **Dynasties**: Track prominent families across generations

**Example**: An ambitious citizen from a plebeian family may work hard to become equites, then senator, creating a political dynasty.

### Social Classes & Mobility

Roman society has distinct classes with different rights and opportunities:

| Class | Mobility | Special Features |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| Slaves | Very Low (10%) | High revolt risk, no rights |
| Plebeians | Low (30%) | Limited voting power, common workers |
| Equites | Medium (50%) | Business bonuses, merchant class |
| Patricians | Medium (20%) | High political power, land owners |
| Senators | Low (10%) | Corruption access, political elite |

**Social Mobility Factors**:
- Education improves class advancement chances
- Wealth enables purchasing higher status
- Merit-based promotion through military or civil service
- Nepotism provides shortcuts (for better or worse)

### Hierarchy of Needs

Citizens have complex, layered needs (inspired by Maslow):

1. **Basic Survival**: Food, water, shelter
2. **Safety**: Security, healthcare, fire protection
3. **Social**: Entertainment, religion, community
4. **Esteem**: Status symbols, recognition, achievement
5. **Self-Actualization**: Education, culture, philosophy

**Consequences of Unmet Needs**:
- Poverty + Unemployment = Crime
- High taxes + Corruption = Protests
- No opportunity + Danger = Emigration
- Severe inequality + Oppression = Revolution

**Strategy**: Meet basic needs first, then progressively address higher needs as your city prospers.

### Crime & Justice

A realistic crime and justice system:

**Crime Types**:
- **Petty Theft**: Caused by poverty and opportunity
- **Organized Crime**: Flourishes under corruption and weak law enforcement
- **Fraud**: Enabled by greed and lax regulation
- **Murder**: Desperation, revenge, or madness
- **Treason**: Foreign influence or severe oppression

**Law Enforcement**:
- Investigation system tracks criminals
- Court trials determine guilt
- Punishments: Fines, prison, exile, execution
- Wrongful convictions are possible (affects justice rating)
- Mob justice occurs if official law is weak

### Disease & Health

Disease spreads realistically:

**Diseases**:
- **Plague** (60% mortality): Spread by rats, very fast contagion
- **Cholera** (50% mortality): Bad water source, fast spread
- **Malaria** (30% mortality): Mosquitos near water, medium spread
- **Influenza** (10% mortality): Person-to-person, very fast spread

**Healthcare Responses**:
- Build hospitals (70% effective but limited capacity)
- Quarantine infected areas (80% effective, severe economic impact)
- Improve medicine quality (expensive but saves lives)
- Public health education (30% reduction in spread)

**Mental Health**:
- Stress from overwork, poverty, war, disasters
- Affects productivity and crime rates
- Build theaters, baths, forums to reduce stress

## Political & Diplomatic Systems

### Internal Politics

Your city has competing factions with different agendas:

- **Military Faction**: Wants conquest, defense spending, glory
- **Merchants**: Want trade, low taxes, infrastructure
- **Religious**: Want temples, festivals, moral laws
- **Populares** (Populists): Want welfare, land reform, democracy
- **Optimates** (Conservatives): Want tradition, aristocracy, stability

**Political Intrigue**:
- Conspiracies and plots
- Rare assassinations
- Coups if instability is high
- Bribes and blackmail
- Elections (vulnerable to vote-buying and rigging if corrupt)

**Strategy**: Balance faction demands or support one strongly. Ignoring factions leads to unrest.

### Diplomacy & Foreign Relations

Build relationships with neighboring cities and tribes:

**Relationship Factors**:
- Trade volume (+20%)
- Cultural similarity (+15%)
- Military threat (-30%)
- Historical conflicts (-25%)
- Gifts and tributes (+20%)
- Shared enemies (+30%)

**Diplomatic Actions**:

1. **Treaties**:
   - Trade agreements (reduced tariffs, exclusive goods)
   - Military alliances (mutual defense, shared intelligence)
   - Non-aggression pacts (peace, reduced military costs)
   - Cultural exchanges (knowledge, prestige, tourism)

2. **Espionage**:
   - Intelligence gathering
   - Sabotage enemy infrastructure
   - Assassination (very risky!)
   - Steal technology
   - Counter-intelligence to protect yourself

3. **Warfare**:
   - Requires justification (casus belli)
   - War exhaustion affects morale
   - Sieges take time and cause attrition
   - Naval warfare for coastal cities
   - Guerrilla resistance in occupied territories
   - War crimes damage reputation

### Empire Relations

Manage your relationship with Rome:

**Favor System**:
- Complete imperial requests to gain favor
- Failures have severe consequences
- Exceptional service earns rewards:
  - Titles (Proconsul, Legate, Prefect)
  - Land grants
  - Trade monopolies
  - Political appointments

**Punishments for Failure**:
- Reduced autonomy
- Higher tribute demands
- Imperial garrison imposed
- Governor replaced (game over!)

**Imperial Succession**:
- New emperors change policies
- Civil wars occasionally erupt
- Choose sides carefully—risky but potentially very rewarding

## Strategic Depth & Long-term Planning

### Infrastructure Maintenance

Buildings don't last forever:

- Roads deteriorate and need repaving
- Aqueducts require regular maintenance
- Buildings age and become less efficient
- Neglect causes catastrophic failures

**Infrastructure Upgrades**:
- Roads: Dirt → Paved → Stone → Marble
- Aqueducts: Basic → Improved → Advanced
- Walls: Wooden → Stone → Reinforced
- Each level costs more but provides better performance

**Budget Planning**: Set aside 10-15% of income for maintenance.

### Technology Tree

Research and innovation drive progress:

**Research Requirements**:
- Libraries and academies
- Ongoing funding
- Random breakthroughs (rare)
- Tech diffusion through trade

**Technology Categories**:

1. **Agriculture**: Crop rotation, irrigation, fertilization, selective breeding
2. **Construction**: Concrete, arches, domes, urban planning
3. **Military**: Tactics, siege weapons, armor, logistics
4. **Medicine**: Surgery, pharmacology, sanitation, anatomy
5. **Administration**: Bureaucracy, census, law codes, postal system

**Benefits**:
- Better productivity
- Unlock unique buildings
- Military superiority
- Attract more immigrants

**Example**: Research "Concrete" to unlock the Pantheon and double building durability.

### Environmental Challenges

Nature is both friend and foe:

**Climate**:
- Seasons affect farming yields
- Droughts reduce crop production
- Floods can destroy fields but also enrich soil
- Harsh winters increase fuel needs

**Natural Disasters**:
- Earthquakes (unpredictable, destroys buildings)
- Fires (spread rapidly, preventable with fire posts)
- Locust swarms (destroy crops, occasional warnings)
- Volcanic eruptions (rare but devastating)

**Environmental Degradation**:
- Deforestation causes soil erosion and less rainfall
- Overfishing depletes fish stocks (recover slowly)
- Pollution affects health and appeal

**Sustainability**:
- Renewable resource management
- Conservation policies
- Balance short-term profit vs long-term viability

## Dynamic Events & Narratives

### Event System

Experience unique stories and challenges:

**Event Categories**:
- **Political** (20%): Conspiracies, elections, scandals
- **Economic** (25%): Market changes, trade opportunities, crashes
- **Social** (20%): Festivals, unrest, movements
- **Military** (15%): Threats, mercenary offers, invasions
- **Supernatural** (10%): Omens, miracles, curses
- **Personal** (10%): Individual citizen stories

**Event Chains**:
- Your choices trigger follow-up events
- Consequences can be immediate, delayed, or cascading
- Some outcomes are unexpected

**Example Event Chain**:
1. A charismatic demagogue emerges (choice: support or oppose)
2. If supported: Gains political power, popular reforms
3. Later: Reveals authoritarian tendencies
4. Final choice: Tolerate dictatorship or risk civil war

## Multiple Victory Paths

Choose how to win:

### Economic Victory
- Treasury: 100,000 denarii
- Trade dominance in region
- Wealth per capita: 1,000

**Strategy**: Focus on trade, production chains, and banking. Maintain peace to maximize commerce.

### Military Victory
- Conquer all rival cities
- Reputation: "Feared"
- Command 5 legions

**Strategy**: Build strong military, research military tech, maintain veteran troops. Expensive but glorious.

### Cultural Victory
- Build 10 grand monuments
- Prosperity rating: 90+
- Cultural influence spreads to neighbors

**Strategy**: Invest in arts, education, and impressive architecture. Attract tourists and scholars.

### Political Victory
- Become Emperor of Rome
- Reform the Empire
- Found a lasting dynasty

**Strategy**: Accumulate political favor, win elections, eliminate rivals through intrigue.

### Hybrid Victories

You can pursue multiple victory paths simultaneously for unique playthroughs.

## Difficulty Modes

### Story Mode
- **Challenge**: Medium
- **Features**: Guided progression, tutorial hints, forgiving failures
- **Best For**: First-time players, narrative focus

### Survival Mode
- **Challenge**: Hard
- **Features**: Minimal starting resources, hostile environment, random conditions
- **Best For**: Experienced players seeking challenge

### Sandbox Mode
- **Challenge**: Custom
- **Features**: Creative freedom, optional challenges, toggleable disasters
- **Best For**: Experimentation and casual play

### Grand Strategy Mode
- **Challenge**: Very Hard
- **Features**: All systems active, complex AI, long-term planning required
- **Best For**: Strategy enthusiasts, maximum depth

## Tips for Deep Strategic Play

1. **Diversify Your Economy**: Never rely on one resource or trade partner
2. **Invest in Education**: Educated citizens are more productive and innovative
3. **Balance Faction Interests**: Keep all major factions moderately happy
4. **Plan for Disasters**: Maintain emergency funds and redundant infrastructure
5. **Think Long-term**: Today's shortcuts create tomorrow's crises
6. **Adapt to Change**: Markets shift, emperors change, climates vary
7. **Use Espionage Wisely**: Information is power, but getting caught has consequences
8. **Monitor Social Indicators**: Crime, health, and happiness predict bigger problems
9. **Technology Matters**: Early research investment pays off exponentially
10. **Multiple Paths to Glory**: There's no single "right" way to build Rome
